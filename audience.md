# Audience

Teachers and researchers at a university, mainly in biology, medicine and
technology, though the material should work for any faculty.

## What we assume
- They are experts in their own field and can judge whether a result makes sense
  *in their field*. We use this a lot: verification relies on domain knowledge.
- They use a computer every day: files, folders (in a file browser), Office,
  web, email, perhaps Excel formulas.
- Many have used a chat LLM (ChatGPT, Claude.ai, Copilot) for text.

## What we do NOT assume
- The command line. Never assumed, introduced in *Terminal survival kit*.
- Programming in any language. Biologists may have run an R script someone else
  wrote; we do not depend on it.
- git, GitHub, Markdown, HTML.
- Statistics beyond what their own field uses.

## Consequences
- Every unit has to work for someone who has never opened a terminal.
- Explanations of code go only as deep as needed to judge the result ("this line
  removes samples with fewer than 10 reads. Is that what you want?").
- Examples come from several fields; biology-specific material lives in tracks.
- Participants are working staff with little time. Units must produce something
  useful for their own job, preferably with their own data (when the privacy
  rules allow it).
- Expect a big spread in confidence. Installation instructions (*Accounts and installation*) must be
  step by step, with screenshots for Windows and Mac.
