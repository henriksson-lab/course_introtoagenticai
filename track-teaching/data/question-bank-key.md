# Describing data: answer key (PRIVATE: do not publish)

## 1. Mean or median for skewed data  [q01; centre; easy]

Reaction times in a psychology experiment have a long tail to the right: most people answer in about 0.5 s, a few take 3 to 4 s.

Which single number best describes a *typical* reaction time?

- A) The mean: wrong. The few very slow answers pull the mean upwards, so it is larger than most people's times.
- B) The median: **correct**. Half of the times are below it and half above, whatever the tail does.
- C) The mode: wrong. For a continuous measurement, almost every value is unique, so the mode is unstable.
- D) The range: wrong. The range describes spread, not a typical value.

*Feedback:* For skewed data, the median describes the typical value; report the mean too if a later analysis needs it.

## 2. Median of five values  [q02; centre; easy]

Five seedlings have heights (mm) 3, 8, 5, 12 and 7.

What is the median height in mm?

**Answer:** 7 (margin 0)

*Feedback:* Sort the values: 3, 5, 7, 8, 12. The middle value is 7.

## 3. Mean of five values  [q03; centre; easy]

Five students scored 4, 6, 6, 9 and 10 points on a quiz.

What is the mean score?

**Answer:** 7 (margin 0)

*Feedback:* The sum is 35, and 35 / 5 = 7.

## 4. Which error bar  [q04; spread; medium]

A figure shows the mean height of plants in each treatment. The caption says the error bars "show how much individual plants vary".

Which quantity should the error bars show?

- A) The standard deviation (SD): **correct**. The SD describes the spread of individual values around the mean.
- B) The standard error of the mean (SE): wrong. The SE describes how precisely the mean is estimated. It shrinks as more plants are measured, even if the plants vary just as much.
- C) A 95 % confidence interval for the mean: wrong. A confidence interval is about the mean, not about individual plants.
- D) The full range, from the smallest to the largest plant: wrong. The range shows variation but depends on a single extreme plant in each group, and grows with sample size.

*Feedback:* SD for "how much individuals vary"; SE or a confidence interval for "how well we know the mean". Always say which one in the caption.

## 5. Standard error from SD and n  [q05; spread; medium]

A sample of 36 patients has a standard deviation of 12 mmHg for systolic blood pressure.

What is the standard error of the mean, in mmHg?

**Answer:** 2 (margin 0.01)

*Feedback:* SE = SD / sqrt(n) = 12 / sqrt(36) = 12 / 6 = 2.

## 6. A plot for small groups  [q06; plots; medium]

You have measured 8 animals in each of 5 groups and want a plot that lets readers compare the groups.

Which plot is the best choice?

- A) A bar chart of the group means: wrong. With 8 values per group, a bar hides the data: two very different distributions can have the same bar.
- B) A dot plot showing every animal, with the group median marked: **correct**. Every value is visible, so readers can see spread, overlap and outliers themselves.
- C) A pie chart of the group totals: wrong. A pie shows parts of a whole. The groups are not parts of a whole, and totals depend on group size.
- D) A 3D bar chart of the group means: wrong. The third dimension adds nothing and distorts the heights of the bars.

*Feedback:* For small samples, show the data points. Add a box plot or a mean with an interval on top if you like.

## 7. Distances on a log axis  [q07; plots; easy]

A plot has a logarithmic (base 10) y axis.

On this axis, the distance from 10 to 100 is the same as the distance from:

- A) 100 to 190: wrong. That would be true on an ordinary (linear) axis, where equal differences get equal distances.
- B) 100 to 1000: **correct**. On a log axis, equal ratios get equal distances. Both steps are a factor of 10.
- C) 0 to 10: wrong. Zero cannot be shown on a log axis: the logarithm of 0 is not defined.
- D) 50 to 100: wrong. That step is a factor of 2, not 10, so it is shorter.

*Feedback:* Log axes turn equal ratios into equal distances. Useful for data that spans several orders of magnitude.

## 8. Percentage change  [q08; comparison; medium]

The number of students who passed a course went from 40 one year to 50 the next.

By what percentage did the number increase? Enter a number without the % sign.

**Answer:** 25 (margin 0)

*Feedback:* The change is 10, and 10 / 40 = 0.25, so 25 %. (Dividing by the new value, 50, gives 20 %: a common mistake.)

## 9. Correlation and cause  [q09; correlation; hard]

Across the months of several years, a town's ice-cream sales and the number of drownings have a correlation of r = 0.9.

Which statement is best supported?

- A) Eating ice cream increases the risk of drowning.: wrong. A correlation alone does not show which way an effect goes, or whether there is one at all.
- B) A third factor, such as warm weather, may drive both.: **correct**. Warm months bring both more ice cream and more swimming. A shared cause produces a strong correlation without any direct effect.
- C) Ice-cream sales explain 90 % of the variation in drownings.: wrong. The share of variation explained is r squared (0.81), not r, and even that says nothing about cause.
- D) The correlation is too weak to mean anything.: wrong. r = 0.9 is a strong correlation. The problem is interpretation, not strength.

*Feedback:* A strong correlation is a reason to look for an explanation, not an explanation. Look for shared causes before you claim an effect.

## 10. The line in a box plot  [q10; plots; easy]

In a standard box plot, what does the line inside the box show?

- A) The mean: wrong. A common mix-up. The standard box plot is built from quartiles; some programs add the mean as a separate symbol.
- B) The median: **correct**. The box runs from the first to the third quartile, and the line marks the median (the second quartile).
- C) The mode: wrong. Box plots do not show the mode.
- D) The midpoint between the smallest and largest value: wrong. That is the mid-range. It depends only on the two extreme values.

*Feedback:* Box = middle half of the data (first to third quartile); line = median; whiskers = the rest, up to a limit; points beyond = possible outliers.
